    Tenancy: sharing scarce GPUs with KAI and MIG

    The problem

    GPUs never cover demand, so a partner must answer three questions for every customer team (tenant):

    1. How much is guaranteed? A quota no one can take away.
    2. What can be borrowed? Idle GPUs of another team, given back when it needs them.
    3. What about a job that needs several GPUs at once? A distributed training job is useless with half its GPUs: it should start whole or not at all (gang scheduling).

    Part 1 only had a fixed cap per team on a shared L4.

    What I used

    • KAI Scheduler (NVIDIA, v0.18.6), on both sites: per-team queues with a guaranteed quota, borrowing, take-back (reclaim) and gangs.
    • MIG (Multi-Instance GPU) on the H200: cuts one GPU into isolated slices, each with its own memory and compute, so two tenants can share one GPU without seeing each other.
    Queue Paris (simulated GPUs) Finland (real H200s)
    team-a quota 4 quota 1
    team-b quota 4 quota 1

    What happened

    Test Where Result
    Borrowing Paris, simulated team-a used 8 GPUs on a quota of 4 while team-b was idle
    Reclaim Paris, simulated team-b asked for 4: KAI took back exactly team-a's 4 borrowed GPUs in 5 s, nothing in quota touched
    All or nothing Paris and Finland A gang of 6 on 4 free GPUs, and of 3 on 2 real H200s: no pod started, no GPU wasted
    Gang on real GPUs Finland 2 pods, 1 H200 each, both started in the same second
    MIG Finland One H200 cut into 2 slices in 33 s, no reboot; two tenants, one slice each, about 70 GB each

    Two lessons

    • Two pods on one server don't use NVLink by default. Between the two gang pods, data moved at 2.24 GB/s over the pod network, against 351 GB/s inside one pod holding both GPUs. A tenant's 2-GPU job should run as one pod.
    • KAI won't mix MIG slices and whole GPUs on one server. A pod asking for the second, whole H200 was refused. So a partner splits servers: MIG servers and whole-GPU servers (ADR 0008).

    What was simulated

    Paris's 8 GPUs are fake (two KWOK nodes running no containers). KAI's decisions about them are real. Everything in Finland ran on the real H200s.

    In one sentence

    KAI gave each team a guaranteed share, lent idle GPUs and took them back exactly, never started half a job, and MIG let two tenants share one H200 safely; the run also showed two limits a partner has to design around.

    Setup

    • KAI v0.18.6, one Application per site with that site's queues (gitops/apps/{par,fin}/scheduling/kai-scheduler.yaml). Only GPUs count against quotas. A workload opts in with schedulerName: kai-scheduler and the label kai.scheduler/queue; one annotation, kai.scheduler/batch-min-member, makes a Job a gang.
    • Paris, SIMULATED: two KWOK nodes with four fake H200s each from run-ai/fake-gpu-operator 0.2.1. Only its KWOK path is installed, because the real GPU Operator strips the fake labels from real nodes; KWOK and the fake operator are vendored and pinned to the CPU worker (what is simulated).
    • Finland: MIG manager with my layout h200-split (GPU0 as two 3g.71gb, GPU1 whole), applied when the node gets nvidia.com/mig.config=h200-split. A fallback rtxpro-split exists for 2x RTX PRO 6000; unused. Layouts checked first with mig-parted v0.15.1's own loader (capture).

    The whole-GPU pod KAI refused

    team-a's pod asking nvidia.com/gpu: 1 (GPU1, whole) stayed Pending, and again when resubmitted alone within quota:

    Full capture. A rule in KAI v0.18.6 (pkg/scheduler/api/node_info/node_info.go): on a node with nvidia.com/mig.strategy=mixed, a non-MIG task gets a fit error, although the GPU Operator still advertises the whole GPU (finding).

    Issue met

    Prometheus fired TargetDown and KubeletInstanceUnreachable for the KWOK nodes' fake kubelets; the Paris kubelet ServiceMonitor now drops eu1-par-sim-gpu-.* (capture).

    Limits

    • One GPU node: no gangs across nodes, no topology-aware placement.
    • No fault injected between MIG slices; their isolation is from NVIDIA's documentation.
    • KAI's fairness over time needs its Prometheus integration, which is off.
    • Two tenants and 8 simulated GPUs, not hundreds of nodes.
